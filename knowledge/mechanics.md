
# IB Physics SL — Mechanics

## Topic: Motion

### Core concepts
- Displacement and distance
- Velocity and speed
- Acceleration
- Motion under constant acceleration
- Free fall and gravitational acceleration

### Essential relationships
- v = u + at
- s = ut + 1/2 at²
- v² = u² + 2as

### Prerequisite knowledge
- Rearranging equations
- Understanding positive and negative directions
- Interpreting velocity-time graphs
- Working with SI units

### Common misconceptions
1. At the highest point of vertical motion,
   velocity is zero but acceleration is not zero.

2. Negative acceleration does not always mean
   that an object is slowing down.

3. Distance and displacement are not equivalent.

4. The direction of acceleration is determined
   by the net force, not necessarily by velocity.

### Teaching strategy
1. Ask the student to identify the physical situation.
2. Ask which quantities are known and unknown.
3. Check whether the student understands
   the relevant physical concepts.
4. Help the student choose a suitable relationship.
5. Guide the student through the calculation.
6. Ask the student to verify units and plausibility.

### Adaptive support
If the student cannot identify the relevant concept:
- Explain the concept using a simple example.
- Generate an easier related problem.
- Guide the student through that problem.
- Return to the original task.

### Example: Vertical motion
A ball is thrown vertically upward.

Ask:
- What is the velocity at the highest point?
- What is the direction of acceleration?
- Which kinematic relationship could be useful?
